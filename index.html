China's assertiveness · 中国的强硬外交姿态

Confrontation over time: China's threats, demands, and representations 对抗态势：中国的威胁、要求与交涉

Active threat 迫切威胁
The spokesperson makes an explicit threat that names a concrete coercive action China will or may take — countermeasures, sanctions, expulsions, and the like. A stronger, more specific coding than generic "Threat" (a strict subset of it). Rises sharply after 2017 and peaks in 2020 (COVID-era US–China confrontation, Hong Kong, Xinjiang) before surging again in 2024–25. 迫切威胁：发言人明确威胁中国将采取或可能采取具体的强制行动——反制措施、制裁、驱逐等，是比一般"威胁"更强、更具体的编码（属其严格子集）。2017年后显著上升，2020年（新冠疫情、香港、新疆）及2024–25年达到高峰。
Threat 威胁
The spokesperson's answer makes a threat of any kind toward another party — from explicit warnings of countermeasures and retaliation to firm declarations that China "will respond" or "take necessary measures." The broadest threat category; "Active threat" is the subset that names a concrete action. 威胁：发言人的回答对另一方发出任何形式的威胁——从明确警告反制、报复，到坚称中国"将作出回应"或"采取必要措施"。这是最宽泛的威胁类别，"迫切威胁"是其中点明具体行动的子集。
Demand 要求
The spokesperson makes a strong, active demand of another party — "demands that," "must," "must immediately stop." The strongest of the demand–urge–request hierarchy (these three are mutually exclusive; only the strongest is coded). 要求：发言人对另一方提出强硬、主动的要求——"要求"、"必须"、"必须立即停止"。这是"要求—敦促—请求"强度序列中最强的一级（三者互斥，只编码最强的一级）。
Urge 敦促
The spokesperson urges or calls on another party to act — "we urge," "we call on," "China calls on all parties to…." A moderate form of pressure, less forceful than a demand. Mutually exclusive with Demand and Request. 敦促：发言人敦促或呼吁另一方采取行动——"我们敦促"、"我们呼吁"、"中国呼吁各方……"。力度中等，弱于"要求"。与"要求""请求"互斥。
Request 请求
The spokesperson makes a simple request of another party — "we hope the relevant side will…," "China requests that…." The mildest form of asking. Mutually exclusive with Demand and Urge. 请求：发言人对另一方提出简单请求——"希望有关方面……"、"中国请求……"。这是最温和的一类。与"要求""敦促"互斥。
Representations (China) 交涉
China makes formal diplomatic representations — the spokesperson says China has lodged a formal objection or protest with a foreign government through official channels, or summoned its ambassador. A standard, institutionalised tool of diplomatic pushback. 交涉：中国正式提出外交交涉，即通过官方渠道向外国政府提出抗议或异议，或召见其大使。这是一种制度化的外交反制手段。
Attacked abroad 海外遇袭 (map only)
A physical or cyber attack on Chinese citizens, workers, buildings, companies, or interests abroad (outside mainland China and Hong Kong) — kidnappings, shootings, bombings, hacking. This is the one category where China is the target rather than the actor, so it is kept off the assertiveness chart above and shown only on the map below. Excludes verbal criticism, economic measures (sanctions/tariffs), and incidents inside China. Concentrated around major overseas-security episodes (2008, 2014–17). 海外遇袭：中国公民、工人、建筑、企业或利益在海外（中国大陆和香港以外）遭受人身暴力或网络攻击——绑架、枪击、爆炸、黑客攻击。这是唯一以中国为受害方而非行为方的类别。不包括口头批评、经济措施（制裁/关税）及中国境内事件。多集中于重大海外安全事件（2008、2014–17年）。

Note: a single exchange can be coded for multiple categories. Counts reflect press conference exchanges, not real-world incidents. The corpus covers 2003–2026; 2026 is partial.

About this site · 关于本站

Data and methods 数据与方法

This site draws on the Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs Press Conference Corpus — a structured dataset of MFA spokesperson Q&A sessions from October 2002 to the present, covering over 35,000 individual exchanges.

Geographic locations in spokesperson answers were extracted using named entity recognition and manually validated. Country mentions reflect the text of answers only; China itself is excluded from geographic counts as it appears in almost every response.

The international order trend data tracks monthly frequency of the term 国际秩序 (guójì zhìxù) in spokesperson answers, with VADER sentiment scoring.

Original data source:
Mochtak, M., & Turcsanyi, R. Q. (2021). Studying Chinese foreign policy narratives: Introducing the Ministry of Foreign Affairs press conferences corpus. Journal of Chinese Political Science, 26(4), 743–761.
Data has been updated and coded further by Jamie Gruffydd-Jones.

Spokesperson biographical data:
Villegas-Cruz, A. M. (2026). Who speaks for Beijing? Insights from a new dataset on China's diplomatic elite. Journal of Current Chinese Affairs. https://doi.org/10.1177/18681026261439122
Chinese Diplomatic Elite Biographical Dataset (CDBD). Education, regional origin, career postings, and Party-congress membership in the spokesperson cards are drawn from this dataset.

Status: Preliminary research. Data and classifications are subject to revision.
